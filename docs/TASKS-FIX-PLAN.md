## A · The repeat — eleven flaws

### A1 · "Does not repeat" does not stop it — **P0**
**Cause.** `updateTask` clears `recurrence_rule` on that one row and leaves
`recurrence_series_id`. The nightly reader picks the newest instance *that still
has a rule*, so it falls back to an older copy and carries on.
**Measured.** 13 of 58 series are in this state; replaying the runner's decision
read-only, **10 would be created again tonight**.
**Fix.** A series becomes a real thing (A12) with a `stopped_at`. Setting
"Does not repeat" stamps it, and the runner skips stopped series. Stop means
stop, from any instance, for ever.

### A2 · Deleting an instance does not stop it — **P0**
**Cause.** Deletion is soft, and the "is there one for today already?" check
counts only rows that are not deleted.
**Measured.** 19–23 generated tasks deleted per day since 18 Sep — and back the
next morning.
**Fix.** Deleting a repeating task asks one question: **"Delete just this one,
or stop it repeating?"** Stopping writes `stopped_at`; deleting one instance
records the day as handled so it is not re-made.

### A3 · Cancelling does not stop it — **P0**
**Cause.** The runner never looks at status.
**Fix.** The same question as A2 on cancel, and a cancelled instance marks its
day as dealt with.

### A4 · Nobody is told they made a daily task — **P0**
> *"if someone accidentally creates a task and doesn't notice that it's a daily
> creation, there's no button to stop that daily creation"*

**Cause.** The Repeats control is one select among ten fields, and after saving
**nothing anywhere says the task repeats**.
**Fix.** Three things: the create dialog states the consequence in words before
saving (*"This will create a new task every day at midnight, assigned to
<name>, until you stop it"*); a **Repeats daily** badge on the card, the row and
the detail panel; and a **Stop repeating** button on the task itself — one
click, from the thing in front of them.

### A5 · No way to see what repeats — **P1**
**Cause.** A series exists only as a column on scattered rows.
**Fix.** A **Repeating tasks** view: every series, its rule, whose it is, which
project, how many copies are open, when the next one arrives, and Stop. For an
admin or coordinator, everybody's; for a member, their own.

### A6 · A series inherits yesterday's edits — **P1**
**Cause.** The next copy is made from the *latest copy*, not from a definition.
Rename one day's task and every future one is renamed.
**Measured.** A series raised as *"making daily report of all pages"* now
generates as *"making daily report of all pages, Weekly report done"*.
**Fix.** A12 — the series carries its own title, assignee, project, priority,
effort and time limit. Editing today's copy changes today's copy.

### A7 · Repeats eat capacity, and capacity is what stops new work — **P0**
**Cause.** Every open copy carries its own effort points, and the runaway guard
allows **14 open copies per series** before it stops. Abdul Moiz: 128 of 132
points are repeat copies.
**Fix, in two parts.**
1. **One open copy per series by default.** If yesterday's is still open, do not
   make another — raise its flag instead ("owed since Monday"). The owner chose
   "always generate" on 2026-09-03 when the alternative was silence; this keeps
   the visibility without the pile-up, and stays switchable per series
   (*Keep every day separately* for work that genuinely is a new job each day).
2. **The tidy-up:** when a series is stopped, offer to close the copies nobody
   has started — "12 untouched copies of this — remove them?" — so capacity is
   returned immediately.

### A8 · A repeat with no due date never runs, and never says so — **P2**
**Cause.** The rule is walked forward from the due date (or start date); with
neither, the series is skipped silently for ever.
**Fix.** The form requires a date when a repeat is chosen; the runner reports
such a series on the Repeating tasks view instead of ignoring it.

### A9 · Generated copies land in Backlog — **P2, needs the owner's answer**
The owner said *"put in their backlog or in a to do"* and left it open. Backlog
means every person moves each copy before they can start it.
**Fix.** Ask once, then apply: **Backlog** (as now) or **To Do**.

### A10 · A stopped series leaves its copies behind — **P1**
**Fix.** Part of A7's tidy-up: stopping asks what to do with the copies that are
still open and untouched.

### A11 · Weekly and monthly are untested in the wild — **P2**
**Measured.** All 56 live series are `FREQ=DAILY;INTERVAL=1`. Weekly and monthly
have unit tests and no field use, so the first person to try weekly is the test.
**Fix.** Cover them in the Repeating tasks view and check both against the
runner before saying they work.

### A12 · The change that makes A1, A2, A3, A6 and A10 possible — **the spine**
A small table, `public.task_series`: the series id, its rule, its defaults
(title, project, assignee, priority, effort, time limit), who owns it,
`stopped_at` / `stopped_by`, and the "one open copy" preference. Backfilled from
the 58 series that exist, so nothing is lost. The runner reads this table
instead of guessing from the last copy; tasks keep pointing at it.

## B · The task page is slow — measured, not guessed

> *"that page is very heavy … taking a lot of time to render … I want this type
> of fast page with filter changing showing an instant change."*

**Measured on production, as the owner, in a real browser:**

| | `/tasks` (default) | `/tasks?range=all` |
|---|--:|--:|
| HTML shipped | **458 KB** | **1.65 MB** |
| Interactive after | **5.5 s** | 4.4 s |
| DOM nodes | 2,622 | **9,345** |
| Server time (TTFB) | 478 ms | 51 ms |

The server is **not** the problem — the database answers in under half a second.
The problem is what is sent.

### B1 · The page ships 1,141 tasks and throws 793 of them away — **P0**
**Cause.** The server reads every task due on or before today **including all
closed ones** (`includeClosed: true`), then the browser hides the ones closed
more than two days ago. Of 1,141 rows sent: **957 are done or cancelled, and 793
are hidden before anybody sees them.**
**Fix.** Apply the rule the browser already applies, in the query: open work,
plus completions from the last few days. Expected ~348 rows instead of 1,141 —
**about a quarter of the payload.** Seeing the whole history stays possible, as
its own request, paged.

### B2 · "All" ships 1.65 MB and 9,345 nodes — **P1**
**Fix.** Page it: the most recent N, with "load more" — and the count stays
honest because the four summary figures already come from their own query.

### B3 · Changing the date range re-runs the whole page — **P1**
**Cause.** The date window lives in the URL, so every nudge is a full server
render of everything above, plus a new payload. That is the "even changing a
filter is heavy" complaint, and it is Rule Zero law 2.
**Fix.** Narrowing inside what is already loaded happens in the browser, in the
same frame; only *widening* past the loaded window asks the server, and the old
rows stay on screen while it answers.

### B4 · Nothing is virtualised — **P1**
**Cause.** Every card in every column is rendered, even off-screen.
**Fix.** Render what is visible plus a margin. This is what keeps it instant
when the division has 10,000 tasks instead of 1,174.

### B5 · A stuck-feeling page during load — **P1**
**Cause.** 5.5 s of hydration with no interactive state; clicks in that window
are lost, which is exactly *"accidentally clicking somewhere else, they get
stuck"*.
**Fix.** Falls out of B1 and B4; verified by re-measuring, not by feel.

**The bar I will hold myself to, and re-measure against:** the default page
under **150 KB** and interactive in **under 1.5 s** on the same probe, and every
filter answering **in its own frame**.

## C · The missing filter — "what did I assign?"

> *"there is no option to see which task is assigned by me … All Assigned Tasks,
> Any Specific Assigned Task, or Self-Created Tasks … If I say Assigned Tasks,
> show a further category: All Assigned Tasks or Some Specific Person's Assigned
> Tasks."*

**Cause.** The toolbar has four filters — status, priority, assignee, project.
"Assignee" answers *who does it*; nothing answers *who asked for it*, although
every row already carries the person who raised it.

**Fix — a "Raised by" control beside "Assignee", exactly as described:**

```
All tasks                    (everything you can see — the default)
Assigned by me  ▸  Everyone                 (I raised it, somebody else does it)
                ▸  Kashif Ayaz              (…and it went to Kashif)
                ▸  Abdul Moiz               (…)
Created by me   (I raised it and I do it — my own work)
Assigned to me  (somebody else raised it, I do it)
```

- Shown to **Super Admin, Admin and Team Coordinator** — a Member assigns to
  nobody, so for them it would always be empty.
- Works with the other filters, not instead of them: *Assigned by me → Kashif*
  **and** status *In Review* answers "what have I given Kashif that is waiting
  for me to approve".
- **Instant** — every row already carries who raised it and who it is for, so
  this is a filter over rows already on the page, never a new request.

## D · The order I recommend

| Stage | What | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | A12 the series table + A1 A2 A3 (stop means stop) + A7's "one open copy" and tidy-up | The team is fighting this daily and capacity is full |
| **2** | A4 (say it at creation, badge it, one-click Stop) + A5 (Repeating tasks view) + A10 | Makes stage 1 usable without anybody being told how |
| **3** | B1 B3 (payload and instant filters) then B2 B4 (paging and virtualising) | The page everybody lives in, re-measured against the bar above |
| **4** | C (the Raised by filter) | Fast to build on the same rows once stage 3 has shrunk them |
| **5** | A6 (edits stop rewriting the future), A8, A9, A11 | Correctness items that stage 1's table makes cheap |

Each stage ships and deploys on its own, with before/after numbers where there
are numbers, and the board and tracker updated as it goes.
